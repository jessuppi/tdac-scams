# Thailand TDAC Scams

This repository documents suspected scams, misleading websites, and deceptive commercial services targeting travelers applying for the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC).

The TDAC is a mandatory online arrival form introduced by Thailand's Immigration Bureau on May 1, 2025, replacing the traditional paper TM.6 arrival and departure card. All foreign travelers entering Thailand by air, land, or sea are generally required to complete the TDAC online no more than three days (72 hours) before arrival, subject to limited exemptions. **The application is completely free when submitted through the official Thai government website.**

Despite the official application being completely free, numerous third-party websites charge travelers for TDAC services. Some may misrepresent their government affiliation, imply that payment is mandatory, imitate official immigration websites, or conceal the availability of the free government service.

## Official TDAC Website

**The only official Thailand Digital Arrival Card website is:**

https://tdac.immigration.go.th/

TDAC applications submitted through Thailand's Immigration Bureau are completely free. Travelers should avoid unofficial TDAC websites that request passport or payment information, especially when their fees, operators, or data handling practices are unclear. Submitting passport or payment information to untrusted TDAC websites may expose travelers to identity theft, phishing, email spam, unauthorized charges, financial fraud, and misuse of sensitive personal data.

## Common Warning Signs

Misleading or fraudulent TDAC websites may exhibit the following warning signs:

- **Government impersonation:** Using official-looking domains, government-style branding, or misleading application pages to falsely claim or imply affiliation with Thai immigration authorities.
- **Misleading fees:** Obscuring service charges or suggesting payment is necessary, speeds up government processing, or provides advantages unavailable through the free official TDAC portal.
- **Deceptive promotion:** Using fake reviews, inflated visitor counts, astroturfing, misleading advertisements, or black-hat SEO tactics to manipulate search visibility and create a false impression of popularity, credibility, or authority.
- **Hidden disclosures:** Burying third-party status, service fees, payment conditions, or the free government alternative in small disclaimers or less-prominent sections of a website.
- **Misleading claims:** Misrepresenting official TDAC requirements, group submission limits, correction options, processing times, or other government portal capabilities.
- **Privacy concerns:** Collecting sensitive passport and travel information without clearly disclosing third-party sharing, marketing use, data retention, or deletion practices.
- **Billing or service issues:** Reported unexpected charges, refund disputes, unresponsive customer support, or failure to deliver promised services.

## Documentation Standards

Each website is evaluated using publicly available information, including website content, pricing, advertisements, screenshots, archived pages, and independent sources.

Listings identify specific concerns, provide supporting evidence, and distinguish verified facts from unconfirmed allegations. Information is reviewed for accuracy and updated when new evidence becomes available.

The Website Comparison table uses ✓ for a documented concern, — for no documented concern, and ? for not yet assessed. Each ✓ must have supporting evidence.

Website operators and other interested parties may submit verifiable corrections or clarifications to ensure the documentation remains accurate by opening a GitHub Issue.
