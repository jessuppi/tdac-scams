# thaitouristvisa.com

Last updated: November 19, 2026

## Misleading Fees

Thaitouristvisa.com charges travelers USD $59.99 for standard Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) processing, $79.99 for expedited processing within four hours, and $109.99 for premium processing within one hour.[^3][^4] However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau provides TDAC registration completely free of charge through its official government portal at `tdac.immigration.go.th`.[^1]

The website promotes faster processing as a benefit of its more expensive packages, despite providing no evidence that purchasing commercial assistance accelerates government processing or grants preferential immigration treatment.[^3] Its Refund Policy also describes TDAC submissions as subject to official approval or rejection, potentially reinforcing the impression that paid processing provides advantages over the free government system.[^4]

Although the operator acknowledges that it is a private intermediary, its disclaimer states that service charges are additional to any applicable government fees.[^3] Because Thailand imposes no government fee for TDAC registration,[^1][^2] this language could mislead travelers into believing that a separate official payment may be required.

These practices raise concerns about whether customers clearly understand that they are purchasing optional administrative assistance rather than a government-required service.


## Deceptive Promotion

Thaitouristvisa.com advertises a 99.2% TDAC approval rate in its Refund Policy without publishing supporting statistics, application volumes, or an independently verifiable methodology. The claim presents its commercial service as highly successful, despite Thailand's Digital Arrival Card being a mandatory arrival-information registration rather than a conventional visa approval process.[^1][^4]

The homepage also promotes a "Certified expert team," professional verification, priority processing, and expert review intended to facilitate faster immigration clearance. However, the website does not identify the certifications held by its staff or provide evidence that its paid assistance results in preferential immigration treatment. Thailand's official TDAC portal already allows travelers to submit their information free of charge and receive their registration details and QR code directly.[^1][^3]

The website further advertises 24/7 customer support, an average response time of less than one hour, and instant live-chat assistance. Its Terms and Conditions, however, describe responses within 24 hours on business days. These differing service descriptions leave customers uncertain about the level of support actually guaranteed.[^3][^5]

These promotional claims may exaggerate the advantages of purchasing commercial assistance. The available evidence does not establish that the operator fabricated its approval statistics, lacks qualified staff, or failed to provide its advertised support.

The website publishes travel and immigration guides attributed to James Walcott, identified as a "Senior Travel Writer." His biography claims more than eight years covering Caribbean travel and immigration, with a professional base between Bridgetown and London. The same name, title, and identical biography appear on the separately branded TDAC website `thailandcard2026.com`.[^12][^23]

Another commercial TDAC website, `thailandarrivalcard2025.com`, previously published travel articles under the same author name. These repeated editorial identities across separate registration services suggest shared content resources or publishing templates, although they do not establish common ownership or coordinated operations.[^24]

The websites do not provide independently verifiable credentials establishing the author's identity or expertise in Thai immigration procedures. Reusing the same editorial profile across multiple commercial TDAC services raises questions about the independence and credibility of their travel guidance, particularly where that guidance promotes paid registration assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

Thaitouristvisa.com identifies Switzerland's Weber GreenTech AG as its operator, listing Florastrasse 17, 8700 Küsnacht, Switzerland, as its business address. Swiss commercial records confirm that the company exists under identification number CHE-109.872.159, but its registered activities concern patents, manufacturing processes, and environmental technologies rather than immigration services. A 2021 World Biogas Association announcement also identifies Weber GreenTech AG as an investor in biogas technology. No independent evidence establishes that the company operates or authorized the TDAC website.[^6][^7][^8]

The website's Refund Policy closely matches one previously published by `thailandarrivalcard2025.com`, including identical service prices, a claimed 99.2% approval rate, refund deadlines, and chargeback warnings. However, that website identified Latvia's Lauksaimniecības SIA "DRUVA" as its operator. These overlapping policies raise questions about the websites' actual operators and their responsibility for customer payments and personal information, without establishing common ownership or unauthorized use of either company's identity.[^4][^9][^10]

The website's legal documents also contain chronological inconsistencies. Its Terms and Conditions claim an effective date of January 2024, while its Refund Policy specifies December 2024. Both refer explicitly to TDAC processing, although Thailand introduced the system on May 1, 2025. Domain-registration records additionally identify February 21, 2026, as the registration date of `thaitouristvisa.com`.[^4][^5][^17][^18]

These discrepancies undermine the clarity of the website's corporate and legal disclosures. However, they do not independently establish fraud, deliberate backdating, or whether older legal templates were subsequently adapted for the TDAC service.

## Misleading Claims

Thaitouristvisa.com repeatedly advises travelers to complete their Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) three to seven days before departure. This advice appears across multiple travel guides, including its 2026 tourist visa guide and travel-document checklist. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau permits official TDAC submission only within three days before arrival, including the arrival date.[^1][^11][^12]

Although a private intermediary may collect travelers' information earlier and submit it later, the website does not clearly distinguish advance preparation through its commercial service from actual registration through the government portal. Its repeated instructions could mislead travelers into believing that the official TDAC system accepts applications seven days in advance.

The website also incorrectly claims that every traveler must submit an individual registration and that no family or group submission option exists. Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly allows families, tour operators, and other authorized representatives to submit information for multiple travelers, with up to ten people included in a single submission.[^1][^11]

These inaccuracies misrepresent the capabilities and submission requirements of Thailand's free government system, potentially making the website's paid assistance appear more necessary or convenient than it actually is.

The website's 2026 entry-card guide also claims that completing digital registration at least 72 hours before departure allows travelers to access automated biometric immigration lanes. It describes passport scanning, facial recognition, fingerprint capture, and immigration clearance through automated kiosks as a routine process for pre-registered visitors.[^15]

However, Thailand's Automated Border Control (ABC) system is restricted to eligible travelers. Under Immigration Bureau Order No. 196/2026, access was expanded to travelers from 33 countries and territories, subject to nationality, entry status, and other conditions. The government's TDAC system now informs applicants whether they qualify for automated gates or must use an ordinary immigration counter.[^1][^16]

The website's guidance incorrectly presents early TDAC registration as sufficient to obtain automated immigration clearance. In reality, submitting a TDAC does not guarantee eligibility, and the official registration window is limited to three days before arrival. These claims could lead travelers to purchase commercial assistance expecting immigration benefits that the intermediary cannot provide.

Thaitouristvisa.com's homepage states that every international visitor and all international passengers must complete a TDAC, without explaining the applicable exemptions. Its travel-document guides also describe the requirement primarily in terms of air arrivals, potentially obscuring its application to land and sea crossings.[^3][^11]

Thailand's Immigration Bureau requires TDAC registration for non-Thai nationals entering by air, land, or sea. However, travelers transiting without passing through immigration control and those entering with a Border Pass are exempt. The Immigration Bureau also confirms that crew members are not required to submit a TDAC.[^1][^20]

By omitting these distinctions, the website could lead exempt travelers to believe they need paid registration assistance while leaving other visitors uncertain about the requirements for land and sea entry.

Thaitouristvisa.com's 2026 tourist visa guide also provides outdated information about Thailand's visa-exemption periods. It describes 60-day visa-free stays for certain nationalities and a 30-day standard for others, without reflecting the revised immigration rules effective September 15, 2026.[^12][^21]

Under the revised rules, nationals of 60 countries and territories, including the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, and most EU member states, generally receive a 30-day tourist visa exemption. Separate bilateral arrangements permit stays of up to 90 days for nationals of Argentina, Brazil, Chile, South Korea, and Peru, while other nationalities have different conditions.[^21]

Because the website continues presenting its earlier guidance as a 2026 visa guide, travelers could incorrectly plan stays exceeding their permitted entry period. This discrepancy is particularly significant for a commercial service marketing itself as a source of professional immigration assistance. The available evidence does not establish whether the operator deliberately retained outdated information or simply failed to update its guides after the policy change.

Thaitouristvisa.com's travel-document checklist and 2026 tourist visa guide incorrectly state that passports must remain valid for at least six months beyond the traveler's intended departure date from Thailand. Both guides describe this as a strict requirement enforced at airline check-in, warning that travelers who fail to meet it will be denied boarding.[^11][^12]

However, official Thai embassy guidance generally requires passports to have at least six months of remaining validity **from the date of entry into Thailand**, not six months after the planned departure date. This distinction matters for travelers whose passports satisfy the official requirement but would fail the website's more restrictive calculation.[^22]

The incorrect guidance could cause unnecessary passport renewals, additional expenses, or disrupted travel arrangements. Travelers should nevertheless verify the specific passport-validity conditions applicable to their nationality, visa category, and airline.

## Privacy Concerns

Thaitouristvisa.com collects sensitive passport information, personal details, travel itineraries, accommodation addresses, and payment information through its commercial TDAC application process. Its Privacy Policy additionally lists account credentials, demographic information, and website usage data among the information it may collect.[^13][^14]

The website's Terms and Conditions state that personal information and passport details are collected and processed exclusively for submitting TDAC applications. However, its separate Privacy Policy permits broader uses, including personalizing website content, analyzing user activity, sending promotional offers, and sharing information with third-party service providers. These disclosures create uncertainty about whether customer information is used solely for immigration registration or also for unrelated commercial purposes.[^5][^13]

More significantly, the Privacy Policy does not specify how long passport records, travel information, or payment-related data are retained after applications are processed. It also does not identify specific retention or deletion schedules for different categories of personal information. Although customers are informed of potential rights to access, correct, or erase their information, the policy provides only general instructions for submitting requests.[^13]

These omissions are concerning because travelers must disclose sensitive identity and travel information to use the service. The available evidence does not establish that the operator has sold customer data, suffered a security breach, or otherwise misused personal information.

## Billing or Service Issues

Thaitouristvisa.com publishes contradictory refund conditions across its Terms and Conditions and Refund Policy. Its Terms state that once an application has been submitted to Thai authorities, the service is considered fully delivered and its fee becomes nonrefundable. However, its separate Refund Policy promises full refunds when Thai immigration authorities officially reject an application after processing.[^4][^5]

The advertised refund guarantee also requires customers to provide official rejection documentation, with notification received within 30 days of processing and a refund request submitted within 60 days of rejection. These conditions may be difficult to satisfy because Thailand's TDAC is an arrival-information registration rather than a conventional visa application requiring discretionary approval.[^1][^4]

More concerningly, the Refund Policy discourages customers from initiating chargebacks through their banks or payment providers. It warns that filing a chargeback without first contacting the company may result in account termination, additional fees, and legal proceedings. The policy does not clarify what additional fees could be imposed or under what circumstances legal action would follow.[^4]

These contradictory provisions leave customers uncertain about their refund eligibility and may discourage them from exercising legitimate payment-dispute rights. The available evidence does not establish that the operator has actually imposed chargeback penalties, pursued legal proceedings, or refused valid refunds.

Three Trustpilot reviews published between April 3 and April 17, 2026, raise additional concerns about the website's commercial practices. All three reviewers criticized the service for charging travelers to obtain Thailand's free Digital Arrival Card. Two also reported encountering the website through sponsored Google search results, suggesting its paid advertising may have contributed to confusion with the official registration portal.[^19]

More seriously, an April 17 reviewer alleged that the operator used customers' payment-card information to make further unauthorized charges after the initial TDAC purchase. This is a firsthand allegation rather than independently verified evidence of fraudulent transactions.[^19]

The complaints reinforce concerns about misleading promotion and payment practices. However, the small number of reviews does not establish how frequently customers experienced problems, and the available evidence does not independently confirm unauthorized charges or identify the party responsible for any disputed transactions.
