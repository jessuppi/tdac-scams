# thailandarrivalcardtourist.com

Last updated: November 1, 2026

> [!NOTE]
> As of November 1, 2026, `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` no longer displays its TDAC application service. The domain currently shows a generic page indicating that it may be available for sale. The findings below document the website's previous activities and historical customer complaints.

## Government Impersonation

The website's historical page title, "Thailand TDAC Digital Arrival Card 2025," prominently used the official name and acronym of Thailand's mandatory arrival registration. A December 2025 Trustpilot reviewer described the website as resembling an official government portal, while another traveler reported mistaking its polished application interface for the government's registration process. Although the website reportedly disclosed that it was not affiliated with the Thai government, its official-sounding branding and presentation could still lead travelers to mistake the private, paid service for Thailand's legitimate Immigration Bureau portal.

## Misleading Fees

On April 8, 2026, U-Tapao International Airport published an Immigration Bureau warning specifically identifying `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` among unofficial websites charging travelers for TDAC registration, which the Thai government provides free of charge. Separately, a traveler reported paying USD $50 through the website for its TDAC submission service. Although this individual account has not been independently verified, it raises concerns about charging substantial fees for a free government registration service.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website promoted expedited TDAC processing as an additional paid service, with customers reporting options costing USD $20 for delivery within four hours and USD $40 for delivery within one hour. These speed-based upgrades implied that paying more could provide a meaningful processing advantage. However, Thailand's official Immigration Bureau portal allows travelers to download their TDAC directly after submitting the free application, which typically takes just 3–5 minutes to complete. The website provided no independently verified evidence that its expedited services offered faster government processing or any official priority. These promotions could mislead travelers into paying extra for speed-related benefits that the free government system already provides.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website reportedly acknowledged its lack of government affiliation on its Contact and legal pages, but this disclosure did not prevent confusion about the paid application process. A traveler who arrived through a sponsored Google search result said they completed the TDAC application fields before encountering the payment request, only afterward recognizing that the website was a private service. This sequence raises concerns about whether the optional, paid nature of the service was sufficiently clear before travelers entered sensitive personal and travel information.

## Misleading Claims

A historical website scan recorded the site's description promoting its service as a way to obtain a "Thailand Visa On Arrival" online. This wording misleadingly associated its paid TDAC application service with obtaining a Thai visa, despite the Thailand Digital Arrival Card being an arrival registration form rather than a visa or authorization to enter the country. Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly confirms that the TDAC does not replace a visa. Such language could confuse travelers about what they were purchasing and whether the service provided any immigration benefit beyond submitting the mandatory arrival form.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collected sensitive personal, passport, and travel information through its private TDAC application process. A December 2025 Gridinsoft security assessment identified three security-provider warnings against the domain, including a malicious classification by CRDF and warnings from Gridinsoft and ScamAdviser. Furthermore, Thailand's Immigration Bureau specifically identified `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` among unofficial TDAC websites in a public warning highlighting the risks of identity theft and misuse of travelers' personal information. Although these findings do not establish that the website actually stole, leaked, or sold customer data, the documented security warnings raise serious concerns about entrusting sensitive passport information to this unaffiliated service.

## Billing or Service Issues

A traveler reported that the website advertised a refund guarantee but failed to respond after they submitted a refund request through its contact form. In a separate December 2025 Trustpilot complaint, another customer alleged that the website neither delivered the promised arrival document nor responded to inquiries. Although these accounts have not been independently verified, they raise concerns about the website's customer support, refund practices, and fulfillment of paid services. The eventual outcomes of the reported disputes remain unknown.
