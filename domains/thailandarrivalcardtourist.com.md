# thailandarrivalcardtourist.com

Last updated: November 1, 2026

> [!NOTE]
> As of November 1, 2026, `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` no longer displays its TDAC application service. The domain currently shows a generic page indicating that it may be available for sale. The findings below document the website's previous activities and historical customer complaints.

## Government Impersonation

The website's historical page title, "Thailand TDAC Digital Arrival Card 2025," prominently used the official name and acronym of Thailand's mandatory arrival registration. A December 2025 Trustpilot reviewer described the website as resembling an official government portal, while another traveler reported mistaking its polished application interface for the government's registration process. These firsthand accounts suggest that the website's branding and application-style presentation could blur the distinction between its private paid service and the Thai Immigration Bureau's official portal.

## Misleading Fees

On April 8, 2026, U-Tapao International Airport published an Immigration Bureau warning specifically identifying `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` among unofficial websites charging travelers for TDAC registration, which the Thai government provides free of charge. Separately, travelers reported paying USD $50 for a single TDAC submission and USD $130 for two registrations, demonstrating that substantial charges were reported by multiple customers. In a September 2025 Trustpilot complaint, another customer alleged that the website advertised a $20 price but charged $69.99, with inquiries about the additional $49.99 going unanswered. Although these individual accounts have not been independently verified, they raise concerns about both charging substantial fees for a free government registration service and potentially misleading customers about the final price.

## Deceptive Promotion

A traveler reported that the website advertised normal TDAC delivery within 24 hours, with an additional USD $20 charge for express delivery within four hours. Another customer described a USD $40 option promising delivery within one hour. These delivery tiers presented faster service as a paid benefit, even though Thailand's official Immigration Bureau portal allows travelers to download their TDAC immediately after submitting the free application, which typically takes just 3–5 minutes to complete. There is no independently verified evidence that the website's paid upgrades accelerated government processing or provided official priority. The contrast raises concerns that travelers were encouraged to pay extra for faster delivery of a document already available directly from the government without charge.

A September 2025 Trustpilot reviewer also alleged that the website displayed a claim of more than 30,000 positive customer reviews. This figure has not been independently verified against an archived version of the website, so the complaint does not establish that the review count was fabricated. Nevertheless, if displayed as described, such a substantial claimed review count could have influenced travelers' perceptions of the service's popularity and credibility.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website reportedly acknowledged its lack of government affiliation on its Contact and legal pages, but this disclosure did not prevent confusion about the paid application process. A traveler who arrived through a sponsored Google search result said they completed the TDAC application fields before encountering the payment request, only afterward recognizing that the website was a private service. This sequence raises concerns about whether the optional, paid nature of the service was sufficiently clear before travelers entered sensitive personal and travel information.

## Misleading Claims

A December 2025 Gridinsoft scan preserved the website's description advertising "Get your Thailand Visa On Arrival online quickly and easily" while also inviting travelers to apply for a "Travell Declaration card." This wording blurred the distinction between obtaining a Thai visa and completing the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). Thailand's Immigration Bureau describes the TDAC as an arrival-information form that travelers must complete separately from any visa requirements, including visa-on-arrival arrangements. Although the recorded wording came from website metadata rather than a verified screenshot of the application interface, it could mislead travelers into believing that the paid TDAC service also helped them obtain a visa.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collected sensitive personal, passport, and travel information through its private TDAC application process. Gridinsoft's December 2025 assessment recorded warnings from two of 28 external security providers: CRDF classified the domain as "Malicious," while ScamAdviser flagged it. Gridinsoft also listed the domain on its own threat list. Separately, Thailand's Immigration Bureau identified `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com` among unofficial TDAC websites while warning about the potential for identity theft and misuse of travelers' personal information. These warnings do not establish that the website actually stole, leaked, or sold customer data, but they raise concerns about entrusting sensitive passport details to an unaffiliated service.

## Billing or Service Issues

A traveler reported that the website advertised a refund guarantee but did not respond to a refund request submitted through its contact form. A December 2025 Trustpilot complaint alleged that no arrival document was delivered and inquiries went unanswered. Another Trustpilot reviewer reported receiving arrival-card documents containing multiple errors and said the website refused a refund despite advertising guaranteed refunds. However, a separate November 2025 reviewer reported that an initially rejected refund was eventually issued after further correspondence. Although these accounts have not been independently verified, and the outcomes of the other disputes remain unknown, they raise concerns about document accuracy, service fulfillment, customer support, and inconsistent refund handling.
