# tdac.info

Last updated: October 28, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The domain `tdac.info` and its page title, "Thailand Digital Arrival Card 2026," prominently use the official form's name. At the top of the homepage, visitors encounter a TDAC application form and a "Start TDAC Application" button before a clear explanation of the site's private role. The website does state "Independent filing service · Not the government portal" below the form and includes a fuller non-affiliation disclaimer near the bottom. Nevertheless, this presentation may lead travelers arriving from search results to mistake the private application process for the official government service at `tdac.immigration.go.th`, particularly before encountering the disclaimers.

## Misleading Fees

The website charges USD $29 per traveler to submit Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC), even though the official government application is completely free. Its homepage prominently promotes a fast, simplified application process and encourages travelers to begin entering personal information before clearly presenting the fee farther down the page. Although the website eventually acknowledges the free government alternative, its presentation may lead travelers to believe that payment is necessary or provides special processing advantages. Thailand's Ministry of Tourism and Sports has explicitly warned travelers against third-party websites requesting payment for TDAC registration.

## Deceptive Promotion

The homepage advertises "Trusted by 10,987 travellers" and "4.9 / 10,987 reviews" without identifying an independent source for those figures. It also promises an "approved QR code" and claims that passport and date-format errors cause most TDAC rejections, while promoting its manual review as protection against those problems. Thailand's Immigration Bureau describes TDAC as an arrival-card submission rather than immigration approval, and entry remains subject to immigration checks. These promotional claims may exaggerate both the service's reputation and the risks of using the free government form, encouraging travelers to pay USD $29 per person for optional assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

The homepage presents a TDAC application form before disclosing its USD $29 per-traveler fee, while the fuller third-party disclaimer appears near the bottom. Its homepage FAQ says filings made seven or more days before arrival are "typically submitted within the day," whereas its separate FAQ page says early applications are queued until the official 72-hour window. Its Terms and Conditions also promise submission "shortly after payment confirmation." The website does not consistently distinguish accepting an application, preparing it, and filing it with Thai Immigration. These conflicting descriptions may leave travelers uncertain about when their applications are actually submitted and what the paid service provides.

## Misleading Claims

The website lists diplomatic and government passport holders as exempt from TDAC, although Thailand's Immigration Bureau requires non-Thai diplomatic passport holders to submit it. Its FAQ claims the official portal cannot edit a submitted card and requires a new application, but the government provides an "Update Arrival Card" function for correcting many details. The same FAQ says families must file separately, despite official group submissions supporting up to 10 travelers. The homepage also warns "No QR code, no entry," whereas immigration provides assistance and arrival kiosks. These misleading comparisons exaggerate the limitations of the free government system and promote the website's paid filing assistance.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collects sensitive passport and travel information and accepts applications up to 12 months before arrival. Its application form promises that personal information is "never stored on our servers after processing," yet its FAQ advertises a "Secure Cloud Backup" that allows agents to retrieve lost TDAC documents using a traveler's name and passport number. Although these statements may concern different records, the website does not explain what information remains stored or for how long. Its published privacy policy primarily addresses comments, contact forms, and cookies rather than passport scans and TDAC submissions. These inconsistencies leave travelers uncertain about how their sensitive identity information is retained and protected.

## Billing or Service Issues

The homepage promises a full refund if Thai Immigration rejects a TDAC because of an error by the service, and money back if the application is not filed before departure. However, its Terms and Conditions list refund eligibility primarily for duplicate payments, payments made in error before submission, or cancellations within 30 minutes before processing begins. They also state that refunds are unavailable after submission to Thai Immigration, without explicitly explaining how the advertised service-error guarantee is honored. This discrepancy may leave customers uncertain about their eligibility for reimbursement if the agency makes a mistake or fails to deliver the promised service.
