# tdac.info

Last updated: October 28, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The domain `tdac.info` and its page title, "Thailand Digital Arrival Card 2026," prominently use the official form's name. At the top of the homepage, visitors encounter a TDAC application form and a "Start TDAC Application" button before a clear explanation of the site's private role. The website does state "Independent filing service · Not the government portal" below the form and includes a fuller non-affiliation disclaimer near the bottom. Nevertheless, this presentation may lead travelers arriving from search results to mistake the private application process for the official government service at `tdac.immigration.go.th`, particularly before encountering the disclaimers.

## Misleading Fees

The website charges USD $29 per traveler to submit Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC), even though the official government application is completely free. Its homepage prominently promotes a fast, simplified application process and encourages travelers to begin entering personal information before clearly presenting the fee farther down the page. Although the website eventually acknowledges the free government alternative, its presentation may lead travelers to believe that payment is necessary or provides special processing advantages. Thailand's Ministry of Tourism and Sports has explicitly warned travelers against third-party websites requesting payment for TDAC registration.

## Deceptive Promotion

The homepage claims to be "Trusted by 10,987 travellers" and displays "4.9 / 10,987 reviews," but does not identify an independent review platform or link to records verifying those figures. It also features traveler testimonials and promises manual passport checks, express QR delivery, and refunds if applications are rejected. Throughout the page, descriptions of the free government application emphasize confusing forms, formatting errors, and the risk of rejection. Without substantiation for its review totals or claimed processing advantages, these promotions may exaggerate the service's reputation and encourage travelers to pay USD $29 per person for optional assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

The homepage begins with a TDAC application form and a prominent "Start TDAC Application" button. Nearby text explains that payment comes on the next page, but does not disclose the USD $29 per-traveler price at this point. The fee appears later in the assisted-filing and pricing sections. A brief "Independent filing service · Not the government portal" notice appears below the form, while the fuller disclaimer and official free portal's address appear near the bottom. This separation may cause visitors to begin the application without fully understanding the service's charges or the availability of the free government alternative.

## Misleading Claims

The website's comparison table describes the official government portal as requiring "One application per traveller — repeat for family members" while promoting its own paid group service for up to six travelers. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau allows up to 10 travelers in one free group submission. The website also claims the official portal takes most first-time users 30 to 60 minutes, whereas the Immigration Bureau estimates approximately three to five minutes per person when the required information is ready. These comparisons exaggerate the official application's limitations and may encourage travelers to pay USD $29 per person for unnecessary filing assistance.

## Privacy Concerns

The website's private TDAC application collects sensitive passport, contact, and travel information and advertises manual reviews of passport scans. It accepts applications up to 12 months before arrival, storing travelers' details until the official submission window opens. Although its application page promises that personal information is not stored on its servers after processing or shared with third parties, its published privacy policy primarily addresses comments, contact forms, cookies, and user accounts. It does not clearly explain how TDAC application records or passport scans are secured, who can access them, or when they are deleted. These omissions make its handling of sensitive traveler information difficult to evaluate.

## Billing or Service Issues

The homepage promises a full refund if Thai Immigration rejects a TDAC because of an error by the service, and money back if the application is not filed before departure. However, its Terms and Conditions list refund eligibility primarily for duplicate payments, payments made in error before submission, or cancellations within 30 minutes before processing begins. They also state that refunds are unavailable after submission to Thai Immigration, without explicitly explaining how the advertised service-error guarantee is honored. This discrepancy may leave customers uncertain about their eligibility for reimbursement if the agency makes a mistake or fails to deliver the promised service.
