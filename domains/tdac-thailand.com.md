# tdac-thailand.com

Last updated: November 9, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The website uses Thailand's official Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) name and acronym throughout its domain, branding, and application process, presenting itself as a TDAC enrollment service. Its application follows a registration sequence requiring travelers to complete a form, pay an application fee, and receive their TDAC, closely resembling the process for obtaining the mandatory government arrival card. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau operates the official TDAC registration portal and provides applications completely free of charge. Although the website includes disclaimers identifying itself as a private assistance service and links to the official government portal, its TDAC-focused branding and paid application workflow may nevertheless create the impression of an authorized registration channel. The concern is the potential implication of official authority through the website's overall presentation, rather than an explicit claim of government ownership.

## Misleading Fees

The website's TDAC application process instructs travelers to "Pay Application Fee" despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing official Digital Arrival Card registration completely free of charge. Its application Terms and Conditions state that the total payment may include both private service fees and charges imposed by immigration authorities, while its refund provisions discuss nonrefundable government assessment fees. The website's separately published Terms and Conditions go further by defining a "government fee" or "arrival card fee" as an amount collected by the issuing government authority from all applicants. These supposed government charges are repeatedly referenced when explaining refund eligibility and credits toward future purchases. However, Thailand does not charge travelers a government fee to submit a TDAC. Although the website identifies itself as a private intermediary, discloses that its assistance carries a service charge, and links to the official government portal, its payment terminology and repeated references to government fees may falsely suggest that some portion of the charge is a mandatory immigration expense. The available evidence does not establish that the operator actually collects a separate government fee, but its published explanations fail to clearly distinguish optional commercial assistance from Thailand's free registration requirement.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website promotes its paid TDAC consultation and application assistance as a professional immigration service, emphasizing convenience for travelers, particularly Chinese nationals who may be unfamiliar with Thailand's registration process. Its homepage advertises TDAC enrollment assistance and email consultation as benefits of its commercial service. However, the application form contains several instructions unrelated to Thailand's arrival requirements. The passport section instructs applicants to provide details for entry into the United States, another instruction refers to mandatory registration with Singapore's SGAC system, and the accommodation section asks for lodging information in Singapore. These errors appear throughout a paid application process that collects travelers' passport and personal information while advertising specialized assistance with Thailand's Digital Arrival Card. Although the inconsistencies do not establish that applications are being mishandled, they directly undermine the expertise and reliability promoted by the operator and may mislead customers about the quality of the assistance they are purchasing.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website's paid TDAC application form includes Terms and Conditions dated June 2020 that leave the operating company's name, tax identification number, and registered office address blank in the section intended to identify the business. Although the document initially provides `info@tdac-thailand.com` as a contact address, it later identifies `info@malaysia-mdac.com` as the contact for the merchant of record, customer service, and refund inquiries. The merchant is described as responsible for collecting payments and issuing invoices, but its legal identity and relationship to the TDAC service are not explained. The application terms also specify Spanish law for contractual disputes without identifying where the operator is established. Separately, the website publishes another set of Terms and Conditions dated January 2024, displayed in Chinese even through its English-language website, which does not resolve these identification gaps. Although the website acknowledges that it is a private, fee-charging service unaffiliated with Thailand's government, these omissions and inconsistencies make it difficult for customers to determine which business they are contracting with, who handles their payments, and who is ultimately responsible for providing the service.

## Misleading Claims

The website describes Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) using terminology associated with immigration permits and government approval rather than routine arrival registration. Its application Terms and Conditions repeatedly characterize the service as obtaining an "Electronic Travel Document" subject to government approval, denial, or subsequent revocation. They also describe an optional priority processing service for an additional fee without clearly explaining whether this merely prioritizes the company's internal handling or offers any benefit in official processing. Separately, the website's published Terms and Conditions advertise a "100% approval guarantee" and discuss refunds for rejected applications. These statements are misleading in the context of TDAC registration, which is a free arrival-information submission rather than a visa or authorization to enter Thailand. The website's homepage further confuses the process by referring to an "MDAC" instead of a TDAC when explaining when travelers will receive their document. Although some of these provisions may originate from generic templates for other countries' immigration services, their inclusion in a paid TDAC application process may lead travelers to believe that government approval, expedited processing, or special guarantees are involved when they are not established features of Thailand's official system.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collects sensitive passport details, personal information, travel itineraries, and contact information through its private TDAC application service. Its application form contains a Privacy Policy dated June 2020 that identifies the data controller by referring to the company named in its Terms and Conditions, although that document leaves the company's identity blank. The policy permits sharing personal information with payment processors, technology providers, and other third parties, including possible transfers outside the European Economic Area, without identifying specific recipients or destination countries. It also provides no specific retention period for travelers' passport and application records. Separately, the website's footer links to another Privacy Policy dated January 2024 that appears in Chinese despite being accessed through the English-language website. This policy repeatedly identifies `singapore-sgac.com` rather than the TDAC website and describes analytics, advertising-related communications, and information sharing with affiliated companies and service providers. Although both policies describe privacy safeguards, the website does not explain their relationship or clarify which provisions govern its TDAC customers. These inconsistencies leave travelers uncertain about who controls their sensitive information, how it may be used or shared, and how long it will be retained.

## Billing or Service Issues

The website publishes conflicting refund conditions across its application form and separately linked policy pages. The application form states that refund requests generally must be submitted within 120 days, that applications not yet submitted to the government may qualify for full refunds, and that approved refunds are returned to the original payment method. Its separately published Refund Policy similarly promises full refunds before government submission, including applications already reviewed by its staff. However, its separately published Terms and Conditions state that customers whose applications have been reviewed but not submitted receive only a credit for the purported government fee, while certain other refunds are issued as credits toward future orders. These provisions are particularly confusing because Thailand's official TDAC registration has no government fee. The application terms also exclude ancillary services from refunds and permit cancellation without reimbursement under certain circumstances. Together, these conflicting policies leave customers uncertain about their refund eligibility, whether they will receive money or store credit, and which conditions actually govern their purchase.
