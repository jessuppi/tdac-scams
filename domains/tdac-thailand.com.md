# tdac-thailand.com

Last updated: November 9, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The privately operated `tdac-thailand.com` adopts the official Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) program's name in its domain and throughout its navigation, branding, and application process. Its homepage uses "TDAC Travel Assist" branding, a "TDAC Enrollment and Travel Consult" heading, and buttons inviting travelers to enroll in TDAC. The advertised process takes customers through consultation, payment, and receipt of their arrival card, while the application form identifies payment as an "Application Fee." This presentation closely associates the website's commercial service with Thailand's mandatory government arrival registration. However, the official TDAC system is operated by Thailand's Immigration Bureau at `tdac.immigration.go.th`, where travelers can submit their information free of charge. The website does publish disclaimers stating that it is not affiliated with Thailand's government, acknowledges its service charge, and links to the official registration portal. Nevertheless, its use of the official program name, TDAC-focused branding, and paid enrollment workflow may lead travelers to mistake the intermediary for an authorized registration channel or believe its services are necessary to complete the government requirement. The concern is the potentially misleading impression of official authority created by the website's overall presentation, rather than any explicit claim that it is operated by the Thai government.

## Misleading Fees

The website's TDAC application process instructs travelers to "Pay Application Fee" despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing official Digital Arrival Card registration completely free of charge. Its application Terms and Conditions state that the total payment may include both private service fees and charges imposed by immigration authorities, while its refund provisions discuss nonrefundable government assessment fees. The website's separately published Terms and Conditions go further by defining a "government fee" or "arrival card fee" as an amount collected by the issuing government authority from all applicants. These supposed government charges are repeatedly referenced when explaining refund eligibility and credits toward future purchases. However, Thailand does not charge travelers a government fee to submit a TDAC. Although the website identifies itself as a private intermediary, discloses that its assistance carries a service charge, and links to the official government portal, its payment terminology and repeated references to government fees may falsely suggest that some portion of the charge is a mandatory immigration expense. The available evidence does not establish that the operator actually collects a separate government fee, but its published explanations fail to clearly distinguish optional commercial assistance from Thailand's free registration requirement.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website promotes its paid TDAC consultation and application assistance as a professional immigration service, emphasizing convenience for Chinese travelers and offering email consultation. However, both language versions of its application contain instructions unrelated to Thailand's arrival requirements. The English-language form instructs applicants to provide passport details for entering the United States, refers to mandatory registration with Singapore's SGAC system, and asks for accommodation information in Singapore. The Chinese-language form contains additional inconsistencies: fields explicitly labeled for arrival in and departure from Thailand use input placeholders referring instead to arrival in and departure from Singapore.

Other errors affect required personal-information fields. In the English application, the nationality field instructs travelers to select their country of birth rather than their nationality, while the residence field directs them to provide birthplace information. The Chinese application likewise uses birthplace instructions for fields labeled as residence information. These discrepancies could confuse applicants whose birthplace differs from their citizenship or current residence.

These errors appear within a paid application process that collects travelers' passport details, contact information, and travel itineraries. Because several incorrect instructions affect required fields, they may lead applicants to provide inaccurate information. Although the inconsistencies do not establish that submitted applications are being mishandled or that the operator deliberately intends to deceive customers, they undermine the accuracy and country-specific expertise promoted by the service and raise legitimate concerns about the reliability of its TDAC application assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website's English-language TDAC application includes Terms and Conditions dated June 2020 that leave the operating company's name, tax identification number, and registered office address blank. However, the Chinese-language homepage footer identifies `青岛商青藤网络科技有限公司` and provides a Chinese business address and telephone number. The website does not explain whether this company is the contracting entity omitted from the application terms or clarify its relationship to the payment processor. The English application identifies `info@tdac-thailand.com` as a company contact but provides `info@malaysia-mdac.com` for the merchant of record, customer service, and refund inquiries. In contrast, the Chinese application identifies `info@tdac-thailand.com` as the merchant-of-record contact. The English application terms also specify Spanish law for contractual disputes without identifying where the contracting company is established. Separately, the website publishes another set of Terms and Conditions dated January 2024 that appears in Chinese even through its English-language website. Although the website acknowledges that it is a private, fee-charging service and its Chinese homepage provides some business identification, these incomplete and inconsistent disclosures leave customers uncertain about which legal entity provides the service, who handles their payments, and who is responsible for resolving disputes.

## Misleading Claims

The website provides inaccurate and potentially misleading descriptions of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). Its Chinese-language homepage incorrectly describes TDAC as a paper form required by Singapore customs authorities and presents the final application step as receiving an approved TDAC. It also lists transit passengers among travelers who generally need to submit a TDAC without explaining that passengers remaining in transit without passing through Thai immigration are exempt. Meanwhile, its English homepage mistakenly refers to an "MDAC" rather than a TDAC when explaining the application timeline. Thailand's Immigration Bureau identifies TDAC as an online arrival-information form replacing the traditional paper arrival card and explicitly states that it is not a visa.

The Chinese-language application contains another potentially consequential error in its health declaration section, where an input placeholder broadly states that travelers entering Thailand must be vaccinated with vaccines approved by Thailand, the World Health Organization, or Thailand's Ministry of Public Health. Thailand does not impose a general COVID-19 vaccination requirement for entry, although specific vaccination requirements apply to travelers arriving from certain yellow-fever-risk areas. The website's unqualified statement may therefore give travelers an inaccurate impression of Thailand's health requirements.

The website's application Terms and Conditions further confuse the requirements by repeatedly describing an "Electronic Travel Document" subject to government approval, denial, or subsequent revocation. They also describe an optional priority processing service for an additional fee without clearly explaining whether priority applies only to the company's internal handling or offers any advantage in official processing. Separately, the website's published Terms and Conditions refer to a "100% approval guarantee" when discussing refunds for rejected applications.

Although some of these statements may originate from generic templates or translation errors rather than deliberate misrepresentation, their inclusion in a paid TDAC service may mislead travelers about which country's authorities administer the arrival card, who needs to submit one, whether government approval is involved, what health requirements apply, and what benefits the intermediary can actually provide.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collects sensitive passport details, personal information, travel itineraries, and contact information through its private TDAC application service. Its application form contains a Privacy Policy dated June 2020 that identifies the data controller by referring to the company named in its Terms and Conditions, although that document leaves the company's identity blank. The policy permits sharing personal information with payment processors, technology providers, and other third parties, including possible transfers outside the European Economic Area, without identifying specific recipients or destination countries. It also provides no specific retention period for travelers' passport and application records.

The application provides inconsistent contact information for exercising personal-data rights. Its English-language Privacy Policy directs travelers seeking to access, correct, or delete their information to `info@malaysia-mdac.com`, while the Chinese-language version instead provides `info@singapore-TDAC.com`. The latter address also appears in the Chinese application's customer service and refund instructions. Neither address uses the TDAC website's domain, and the operator does not explain their relationship to its service or establish which entity receives and processes these requests. Although the addresses could belong to the same operator, the conflicting instructions may make it difficult for travelers to determine whom to contact about their personal information.

Separately, the website's footer links to another Privacy Policy dated January 2024 that appears in Chinese despite being accessed through the English-language website. This policy repeatedly identifies `singapore-sgac.com` rather than the TDAC website and describes analytics, advertising-related communications, and information sharing with affiliated companies and service providers. Although both policies describe privacy safeguards, the website does not explain their relationship or clarify which provisions govern its TDAC customers. Together, these inconsistencies leave travelers uncertain about who controls their sensitive information, how it may be used or shared, how long it will be retained, and how they can exercise their privacy rights.

## Billing or Service Issues

The website publishes conflicting refund conditions across its application form and separately linked policy pages. The application form states that refund requests generally must be submitted within 120 days, that applications not yet submitted to the government may qualify for full refunds, and that approved refunds are returned to the original payment method. Its separately published Refund Policy similarly promises full refunds before government submission, including applications already reviewed by its staff. However, its separately published Terms and Conditions state that customers whose applications have been reviewed but not submitted receive only a credit for the purported government fee, while certain other refunds are issued as credits toward future orders. These provisions are particularly confusing because Thailand's official TDAC registration has no government fee. The application terms also exclude ancillary services from refunds and permit cancellation without reimbursement under certain circumstances. Together, these conflicting policies leave customers uncertain about their refund eligibility, whether they will receive money or store credit, and which conditions actually govern their purchase.
