# tdac-thailand.com

Last updated: November 8, 2026

## Misleading Fees

The website's TDAC application process instructs travelers to "Pay Application Fee" as part of obtaining assistance with Thailand's Digital Arrival Card. Its Terms and Conditions state that the total charge may include private service fees and, where applicable, fees imposed by immigration authorities. The refund provisions also discuss nonrefundable government fees, despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing TDAC registration completely free of charge. Although the website identifies itself as a private service and discloses that assistance carries a fee, its application terminology and references to government charges may confuse travelers about whether they are paying for optional third-party assistance or an official immigration requirement. The website does not clearly distinguish these charges within the cited payment and refund provisions.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website markets itself as a professional immigration service offering paid TDAC consultation and application assistance for travelers entering Thailand. However, its application form contains several instructions unrelated to Thailand's arrival requirements. The passport section instructs applicants to provide details for entry into the United States, another instruction refers to mandatory registration with Singapore's SGAC system, and the accommodation section asks for lodging information in Singapore. These country-specific errors appear within a paid service that collects travelers' passport and personal information while promoting specialized assistance with Thailand's Digital Arrival Card. Although these inconsistencies do not establish that applications are being mishandled, they undermine the professional expertise advertised by the operator and may mislead customers about the accuracy and reliability of its TDAC submission service.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website offers paid assistance with Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) applications and identifies itself as a private service. However, its published Terms and Conditions leave the operating company's name, tax identification number, and registered office address blank in the section intended to identify the business. The same terms provide `info@tdac-thailand.com` as a website contact but identify `info@malaysia-mdac.com` as the merchant-of-record contact for payments and refunds, without naming that merchant or explaining its relationship to the TDAC service. Although the website discloses that assistance carries a service charge, these omissions make it difficult for prospective customers to identify the contracting business and the entity responsible for collecting payments.

## Misleading Claims

The website describes its TDAC service using terminology normally associated with immigration authorizations rather than arrival-card registration. Its Terms and Conditions repeatedly refer to government approval, denial, and revocation of an "Electronic Travel Document" and describe an optional priority processing service for an additional fee. Although these provisions may be generic terms used across different travel-document services, they appear within the TDAC application process without clearly distinguishing them from Thailand's actual requirements. The Thailand Digital Arrival Card is an arrival information form, not a visa or authorization to enter the country, and submitting it does not constitute immigration approval. The website's homepage also mistakenly refers to receiving an "MDAC" rather than a TDAC in its application timeline. Together, these statements may mislead travelers about the nature of TDAC registration, government processing, and the benefits offered by the paid intermediary.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collects sensitive passport details, personal information, travel itineraries, and contact information through its private TDAC application service. Its Privacy Policy identifies the data controller by referring to the company named in its Terms and Conditions, but that document leaves the company's identity blank. The policy permits sharing personal information with payment processors, technology providers, and other third parties, including possible transfers outside the European Economic Area, without identifying specific recipients or destination countries. It also states that personal information may be retained for legal obligations, disputes, and fraud investigations but provides no specific retention period for travelers' passport and application records. Although the policy describes security safeguards and procedures for requesting deletion, these omissions leave customers without clear information about who controls their sensitive data, which organizations may receive it, or how long it may be retained.

## Billing or Service Issues

The website publishes conflicting refund conditions across its application form and separately linked policy pages. The application form states that refund requests generally must be submitted within 120 days, that applications not yet submitted to the government may qualify for full refunds, and that approved refunds are returned to the original payment method. Its separately published Refund Policy similarly promises full refunds before government submission, including applications already reviewed by its staff. However, its separately published Terms and Conditions state that customers whose applications have been reviewed but not submitted receive only a credit for the purported government fee, while certain other refunds are issued as credits toward future orders. These provisions are particularly confusing because Thailand's official TDAC registration has no government fee. The application terms also exclude ancillary services from refunds and permit cancellation without reimbursement under certain circumstances. Together, these conflicting policies leave customers uncertain about their refund eligibility, whether they will receive money or store credit, and which conditions actually govern their purchase.
