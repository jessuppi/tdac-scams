# tdac.in.th

Last updated: October 28, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The domain `tdac.in.th` uses the name of Thailand's official Digital Arrival Card service in its address, page title, and main heading, although the government's actual portal is `tdac.immigration.go.th`. Its homepage explains the mandatory arrival-card requirement, prominently advertises free submission and instant approval, and places an application button alongside those claims. The page includes a disclaimer denying government affiliation, but the combination of official-sounding branding and application prompts may still cause travelers to mistake the privately operated service for an official government website.

## Misleading Fees

The website prominently advertises "TDAC Cost: FREE" and "Instant Approval" alongside its application button, but charges USD $8 (270 THB) for submissions made more than 72 hours before arrival. Although this fee is disclosed farther down the page, paid early submissions are merely held until Thailand's official registration window opens, meaning travelers receive no earlier government processing or approval. This presentation may mislead visitors into believing that payment provides earlier access to the TDAC system or a processing advantage over Thailand's completely free government service.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website prominently advertises "TDAC Cost: FREE" and "Instant Approval" alongside its application button while repeatedly displaying a live discussion widget showing travelers online and more than 1,400 comments. It also features a video from Thai Visa Centre demonstrating the private submission system. These promotional elements may create an exaggerated impression of popularity, reliability, and authority, particularly when displayed alongside application prompts. Although the discussion activity is publicly visible, the accuracy of its visitor counts and engagement figures has not been independently verified.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website currently displays a small disclaimer denying affiliation with the Thai government, but the notice may be easy to overlook alongside its prominent TDAC branding and application prompts. According to Jesse Nickles, this disclosure was added only after intervention by THNIC and Thai law enforcement officials. The website's page title still does not clearly identify it as a private agency or third-party submission service.

## Misleading Claims

The website states that TDAC group submissions support up to 100 travelers, whereas Thailand's Immigration Bureau limits its official TDAC application to 10 travelers per submission. By presenting the higher figure as a feature of the TDAC system without clearly distinguishing its private service from the official government system, the website may mislead travelers about how the official application works.

## Privacy Concerns

The TDAC application collects sensitive passport, contact, and travel information through a private intermediary. Its privacy policy permits sharing personal information with unspecified “sister companies” for marketing without identifying those businesses or their domains. It also provides no specific retention period for TDAC submissions, leaving travelers uncertain about who may receive their information and how long it may be retained.

## Billing or Service Issues

Travelers have publicly reported unexpected charges involving `tdac.in.th` and its associated TDAC submission service. A Trustpilot reviewer complained about being charged USD $50 for a government application that is officially free. On the linked submission service's discussion pages, another traveler reported being charged despite applying within the advertised free 72-hour window, including duplicate charges for two applicants. Although these complaints have not been independently verified, they raise concerns about billing transparency, unexpected charges, and customers' ability to obtain refunds.
