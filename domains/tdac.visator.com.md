# tdac.visator.com

Last updated: October 30, 2026

## Misleading Fees

The website displays three conflicting prices for its TDAC submission service: USD $59.95 on its homepage, USD $69.99 on its application payment screen, and USD $39.95 in its Terms and Conditions. The payment screen labels the $69.99 charge a "TDAC fee," even though Thailand's Immigration Bureau charges nothing for official TDAC submissions. These inconsistent prices and misleading fee terminology create confusion about the actual cost, with no explanation on the respective pages for why the charges differ.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website markets its paid TDAC service using claims such as "Fast Approval," "Priority Processing Included," and "More than 30,000 applications processed." Its About Us page also states that its service fee covers "government-related charges," despite Thailand's official TDAC submission carrying no government fee, as acknowledged in the operator's own Terms and Conditions. The Immigration Bureau allows travelers to submit and download their TDAC directly through its free government portal. The website provides no evidence that purchasing its service grants faster government processing, priority immigration treatment, or other official advantages. These promotional claims may create a misleading impression that payment provides benefits beyond ordinary TDAC submission.

## Misleading Claims

The website describes Thailand's Digital Arrival Card as an "Entry Permit" and advertises a stay of "90 days with a single document," misleading travelers about what the TDAC actually provides. Thailand's Immigration Bureau identifies the TDAC as an arrival registration form, not a visa or authorization to stay for 90 days. Each TDAC covers one arrival, with no renewal required while the traveler remains in Thailand. A new submission is necessary only when entering Thailand again. The permitted length of stay depends on immigration rules and the traveler's entry status, not the TDAC. The website's presentation may falsely suggest that purchasing its submission service provides permission to remain in Thailand for 90 days.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collects sensitive passport information, personal details, contact information, and travel data through its private TDAC application service. However, its Privacy Policy provides no specific retention period for these records, stating only that information is stored as long as necessary or until unspecified legal claim periods expire. The section labeled "Data Retention" does not explain retention practices at all, instead describing information sharing with IT providers, accounting partners, payment processors, and unspecified organizations outside the EU/EEA. These vague and incorrectly organized disclosures leave travelers without clear information about how long their passport and travel records are retained or which external organizations may receive them.

## Billing or Service Issues

Multiple Trustpilot reviewers report paying for TDAC submissions that were never delivered. A January 2026 reviewer stated that Thai immigration officers found no submitted application despite his payment of approximately USD $39. Two other reviewers described paying without receiving their TDAC documents, followed by unanswered emails and refund requests. The website's Legal Disclaimer also warns that chargebacks deemed contrary to its terms may be treated as fraudulent and reserves the right to alert immigration authorities where applicable. These customer complaints, combined with the operator's chargeback policy, raise serious concerns about service fulfillment, refunds, and how payment disputes are handled.
