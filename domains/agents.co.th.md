# agents.co.th

Last updated: October 28, 2026

## Misleading Fees

The website promotes its USD $8 (270 THB) early-submission service as an advantage over Thailand's official TDAC system, which does not accept applications more than three days before arrival. However, paying this fee does not provide earlier government processing or approval. Instead, the private agency collects travelers' information in advance and submits it once the official registration window opens. By presenting this paid scheduling service as a superior submission capability, the website may mislead travelers about what the additional fee actually provides.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website promotes its private TDAC submission portal as a significant improvement over Thailand's official government system, advertising "10x" efficiency gains, unlimited travelers per submission, and support for 76 languages compared with five on the official portal. However, its own description specifies support for 25 to 80 traveler profiles simultaneously, raising questions about its unlimited submission claim. These promotional comparisons may exaggerate the capabilities and advantages of its intermediary service while encouraging travelers and tour operators to use its private platform instead of Thailand's free government application.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website identifies its TDAC portal as a third-party service and discloses an USD $8 (270 THB) fee for submissions more than 72 hours before arrival. However, its homepage promotes early submission as an advantage over Thailand's official government system without clearly explaining that these applications are held by the agency until the official submission window opens. This distinction is explained on its separate TDAC website rather than alongside the homepage's pricing comparison. Travelers may therefore misunderstand whether the fee provides earlier government processing or merely advance collection and scheduling of their information.

## Misleading Claims

The website's comparison table claims that Thailand's official TDAC system does not support resubmissions, presenting this functionality as an exclusive advantage of its private service. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly permits travelers to submit a new application to correct information, with the most recent submission taking precedence. The website also advertises unlimited travelers per submission while elsewhere describing support for 25 to 80 traveler profiles simultaneously, leaving its actual group capacity unclear. These comparisons may mislead travelers about the capabilities and limitations of the free government system.

## Privacy Concerns

The website promotes a private TDAC submission service that processes sensitive passport, contact, and travel information. Its privacy policy permits sharing personal information with unspecified sister companies for marketing purposes without identifying those businesses or their domains. Although the policy also states that personally identifiable information will not be shared with third parties, it separately permits disclosures to affiliates, service providers, and related companies, creating ambiguity about who may receive travelers' data. Furthermore, the policy provides no definite retention period for submitted personal information, leaving travelers uncertain about how long their sensitive records may be stored and how they may be used across the company's services.

## Billing or Service Issues

The company's refund policy offers refunds for canceled trips or TDAC early-submission services canceled more than seven days before arrival, but does not clearly explain how refunds are handled when applications are delayed, processed incorrectly, or charged unexpectedly. On its associated TDAC submission website, a traveler publicly complained about being charged despite applying within the advertised free 72-hour window and reported duplicate charges for two applicants. Although this complaint has not been independently verified, the reported billing problems and gaps in the published refund policy raise concerns about how customers can dispute charges or obtain reimbursement when the service fails to deliver as expected.
