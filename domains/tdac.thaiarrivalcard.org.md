# tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org

Last updated: October 31, 2026

> [!NOTE]
> As of October 31, 2026, the website redirects to a Lovable Trust & Safety takedown notice. The findings below describe previously indexed website content.

## Government Impersonation

The `tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org` subdomain used the official TDAC acronym, while its homepage prominently presented Thailand's Digital Arrival Card as mandatory and encouraged visitors to apply through its private service. The page title was "Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) — Online Application Assistance," and a notice near the top explicitly identified the service as independent and unaffiliated with the Thai government. Despite these disclosures, the official terminology and application prompts could lead travelers arriving from search results to mistake the private service for Thailand's government TDAC portal.

## Misleading Fees

The website's footer described its paid TDAC assistance fee as being "in addition to any official government fees that may apply," although Thailand's Immigration Bureau charges no fee for TDAC registration. The next sentence did tell visitors that they could submit the TDAC directly through the official government portal for free, but the generic government-fee wording still left room for confusion. More seriously, an October 25, 2026 Trustpilot reviewer alleged that the website advertised a USD $10 service fee but charged their card USD $269.97 after payment details were entered. The reported charge has not been independently verified, but the alleged discrepancy raises serious concerns about the transparency of the website's pricing.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website's About page promoted "Fast Processing Options" ranging from standard to "ultra-fast" turnaround times, alongside error checking, 24/7 customer support, and claims of being a "Trusted Service." Its Contact page stated that inquiries typically received responses within 2–4 hours during business hours, qualifying the expectation created by its around-the-clock support claim. However, the website provided no supporting performance figures or independent verification of its processing advantages. The website also failed to clarify whether expedited processing applied only to its own handling of applications or offered any advantage through Thailand's Immigration Bureau. Because the official TDAC portal provides free registration that typically takes just 3–5 minutes per person, these promotional claims could exaggerate the practical benefits of purchasing third-party assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website displayed a brief notice near the top of its homepage identifying itself as an independent service, but that notice did not explain that its assistance was paid or that official TDAC registration was free. These details appeared instead in a lengthy footer disclaimer and a separate About page, neither of which specified the actual service price. Meanwhile, the homepage emphasized the mandatory arrival form and encouraged travelers to apply through its own interface. This separation could lead visitors to begin the application process without fully understanding the optional nature and cost of the private service.

## Misleading Claims

The website repeatedly promised travelers an "approved QR code" by email after submitting their TDAC application. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau describes the QR code as evidence of TDAC submission, not approval to enter Thailand. Because the TDAC is an arrival registration form rather than a visa, and entry remains subject to immigration checks, describing the QR code as "approved" could mislead travelers about what they had actually obtained.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collected sensitive passport information, dates of birth, contact details, and travel data through its private TDAC application service. Its Privacy Policy permitted sharing personal information with third-party providers for payment processing, hosting, email delivery, and analytics, as well as processing data in other countries, including the United States. Its Cookie Policy identified Google for analytics and advertising, Meta as a potential advertising tracker, and NMI (Network Merchants) for payment processing. However, these disclosures do not establish which tracking technologies were actually active. The Privacy Policy also specified no fixed retention period for passport or travel records, stating that information would be kept as long as reasonably necessary, although users could request deletion. This left travelers without a clear timeframe for how long their sensitive records might be retained.

## Billing or Service Issues

An October 25, 2026 Trustpilot reviewer reported blocking their payment card and filing a fraud dispute with their bank after a charge on `tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org` that they said exceeded the advertised service fee. The website's Contact page listed `support@ivisaportal.com` and a general inquiry form but provided no specific instructions for disputing charges or requesting refunds. The same email address also appeared on iVisaPortal's website, which identified ZA Logistics Inc of Houston, Texas, as its operator. This shared contact suggests a connection between the services but does not independently establish ownership of `tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org`. The review does not establish whether a TDAC was delivered or the payment was reversed, and the reported charge has not been independently verified.
