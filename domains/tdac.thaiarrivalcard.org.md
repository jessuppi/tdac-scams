# tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org

Last updated: October 31, 2026

> [!NOTE]
> As of October 31, 2026, the website redirects to a Lovable Trust & Safety takedown notice. The findings below describe previously indexed website content.

## Government Impersonation

The `tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org` subdomain and homepage prominently used Thailand's official Digital Arrival Card name, emphasized that the form was mandatory, and encouraged travelers to apply through its private service. The website also promised delivery of an "approved QR code" by email, potentially suggesting official immigration processing. Although its homepage footer and About page disclosed that the service was privately operated, unaffiliated with the Thai government, and optional, these explanations were separate from its primary application messaging. This presentation could mislead travelers arriving from search results into mistaking the website for Thailand's official TDAC application portal.

## Misleading Fees

The website described its paid TDAC assistance fee as being "in addition to any official government fees that may apply," despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing TDAC registration completely free of charge. This wording could confuse travelers about whether additional government charges existed. More seriously, an October 25, 2026 Trustpilot reviewer alleged that the website advertised a USD $10 service fee but charged their card USD $269.97 after payment details were entered. Although this reported charge has not been independently verified, the substantial alleged discrepancy raises serious concerns about the transparency of the website's pricing.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website's About page promoted "Fast Processing Options" ranging from standard to "ultra-fast" turnaround times, alongside error checking, 24/7 customer support, and claims of being a "Trusted Service." However, it provided no supporting performance figures or independent verification of these advantages. The website also failed to clarify whether expedited processing applied only to its own handling of applications or offered any advantage through Thailand's Immigration Bureau. Because the official TDAC portal provides free registration that typically takes just 3–5 minutes per person, these promotional claims could exaggerate the practical benefits of purchasing third-party assistance.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website displayed a brief notice near the top of its homepage identifying itself as an independent service, but that notice did not explain that its assistance was paid or that official TDAC registration was free. These details appeared instead in a lengthy footer disclaimer and a separate About page, neither of which specified the actual service price. Meanwhile, the homepage emphasized the mandatory arrival form and encouraged travelers to apply through its own interface. This separation could lead visitors to begin the application process without fully understanding the optional nature and cost of the private service.

## Misleading Claims

The website repeatedly promised travelers an "approved QR code" by email after submitting their TDAC application, potentially implying that the arrival card constitutes immigration approval or permission to enter Thailand. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau identifies the TDAC as an arrival registration form, not a visa, and entry remains subject to immigration checks. Separately, the website advertised "24/7 Customer Support," while its Contact page stated that inquiries typically receive responses within 2–4 hours during business hours. These descriptions could mislead travelers about both the significance of their TDAC confirmation and the availability of customer assistance.

## Privacy Concerns

The website collected sensitive passport information, dates of birth, contact details, and travel data through its private TDAC application service. Its Privacy Policy permitted sharing personal information with third-party providers for payment processing, hosting, email delivery, and analytics, as well as processing data in other countries, including the United States. Its Cookie Policy also disclosed advertising trackers associated with Google and Meta. However, the Privacy Policy specified no maximum retention period for passport or travel records, stating only that information would be kept as long as reasonably necessary. These broad disclosures left travelers without clear information about how long their sensitive records might be retained or which external organizations could process them.

## Billing or Service Issues

An October 25, 2026 Trustpilot reviewer alleged that after entering payment details on `tdac.thaiarrivalcard.org`, their card was charged USD $269.97 rather than the advertised USD $10 service fee. The reviewer reported immediately blocking the card and filing a fraud dispute with their bank. The website's Contact page listed `support@ivisaportal.com` and a general inquiry form but provided no specific instructions for disputing charges or requesting refunds. The review does not establish whether a TDAC was delivered or the payment was reversed, and the reported charge has not been independently verified.
