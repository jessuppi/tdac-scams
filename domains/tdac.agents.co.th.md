# tdac.agents.co.th

## Government Impersonation

The website prominently uses the official name of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) service in its page title and branding, with an application button directing travelers to its privately operated submission system. This presentation may cause visitors to mistake it for the official government portal. Although the website includes a disclaimer stating that it is not affiliated with the Thai government and links to the official immigration website, its prominent TDAC branding may still create confusion about the service's identity.

## Misleading Fees

The website prominently advertises "TDAC Cost: FREE" and "Instant Approval" alongside its application button, yet charges USD $8 for submissions made more than 72 hours before arrival. These paid early submissions are merely held until Thailand's official 72-hour submission window opens, meaning travelers receive no earlier government processing or approval. This arrangement may mislead travelers into believing that paying provides earlier access to the TDAC system or a necessary registration benefit. Thailand's Immigration Bureau provides TDAC registration completely free of charge, and the Ministry of Tourism and Sports has explicitly warned travelers against third-party websites requesting payment for TDAC registration.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website prominently advertises free TDAC submissions and instant approval while displaying live traveler activity counters and more than 1,400 comments to reinforce its credibility. It also publishes a "TDAC Scam Alert" page that labels numerous competing services as scams or untrusted operators while presenting its own fee-charging service alongside Thailand's official government portal as a trusted alternative. These self-promotional tactics may create a misleading impression of independent endorsement, established authority, and superior reliability, encouraging travelers to use its private submission service instead of the free government website.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website prominently advertises "TDAC Cost: FREE" and "Instant Approval" alongside its application button, while the USD $8 early-submission fee appears farther down the page. Although a brief disclaimer states that the service is not affiliated with the Thai government and links to the official TDAC portal, the website's main branding and application prompts give much greater prominence to its submission service. This presentation may cause travelers to overlook the private operator's commercial role, the possibility of additional charges, and the availability of Thailand's completely free government application.

## Misleading Claims

The website states that the TDAC system supports group submissions of up to 100 travelers and elsewhere advertises unlimited travelers in a single submission. However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau limits official TDAC group applications to 10 travelers per submission. By presenting its private service's expanded capacity as a feature of the TDAC system itself, the website misrepresents how the official government application works and may mislead travelers into believing its intermediary service offers capabilities unavailable through the official portal.

## Privacy Concerns

The website directs travelers to a private application system that collects sensitive passport, contact, and travel information. Its privacy policy permits sharing personal information with unspecified sister companies for marketing purposes without identifying those companies or their websites. The policy also states that personally identifiable information will not be shared with third parties, creating ambiguity about how information may be shared within its corporate network. Furthermore, it provides no definite retention period for submitted personal information, leaving travelers uncertain about who may access their sensitive data, how it may be used, and how long it will be stored.

## Billing or Service Issues

Travelers have publicly reported billing and customer support problems involving the service. On the website's own discussion pages, one traveler complained about being charged despite submitting within the advertised free 72-hour window, including duplicate charges for two applicants. Another requested assistance obtaining a refund. A Trustpilot reviewer reported problems completing the application and receiving no response from live chat or email support for more than six weeks. These complaints raise concerns about billing accuracy, refund handling, and the reliability of the website's advertised customer support.
