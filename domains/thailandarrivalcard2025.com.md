# thailandarrivalcard2025.com

Last updated: November 11, 2026

## Government Impersonation

The website previously promoted paid Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) registration through a domain closely associated with the official government program. Its homepage prominently described TDAC registration as mandatory and encouraged travelers to complete applications through its own commercial service, potentially creating confusion with Thailand's official immigration portal.

However, the website's About Us page and footer explicitly disclosed that it was a private intermediary, acknowledged its lack of government affiliation, and identified the free official registration service at `tdac.immigration.go.th`.

The concern is the potentially misleading impression created by its branding and application-focused presentation, rather than an explicit claim of government affiliation. As of November 2026, the domain displays a parked page and no longer appears to operate its former TDAC service.

## Misleading Fees

The website previously charged travelers for Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) assistance despite official government registration being completely free. Its homepage advertised a Basic Plan costing $49.99 and an expedited service costing $69.99, with an additional $20 processing option.

However, its separate Refund Policy advertised different prices: $59.99 for standard processing, $79.99 for fast processing, and $109.99 for ultra-premium processing. These inconsistent published prices could leave customers uncertain about the actual cost of the service.

Furthermore, the website's disclaimer stated that its commercial service fees were charged in addition to any applicable government fees, potentially suggesting that Thailand imposes a separate TDAC registration charge.

Although the operator disclosed that it provided optional paid assistance, these pricing inconsistencies and references to government fees risked misleading travelers about the cost of obtaining a TDAC. The available evidence does not establish what individual customers actually paid.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website previously promoted its paid TDAC service using claims of more than 30,000 successful approvals, years of immigration consulting experience, and thousands of completed applications. Its Refund Policy separately advertised a 99.2% approval rate without providing independently verifiable statistics or supporting methodology.

The homepage also displayed favorable customer testimonials and promoted expedited processing, professional application reviews, and round-the-clock support. The identities and experiences of the quoted customers could not be independently verified.

These promotional claims may have exaggerated the advantages of purchasing commercial assistance for a government registration process that is available free of charge and typically takes approximately three to five minutes to complete through Thailand's official portal.

The absence of independent verification does not establish that the operator fabricated its customer testimonials or processing statistics.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website previously identified Lauksaimniecības SIA "DRUVA", a company purportedly registered in Latvia, as its operator. However, its Privacy Policy also referred to Thailand Travel Declaration LLC, while its footer displayed the branding Thailand Agency Travel. The relationship between these different names was not clearly explained.

Furthermore, its Refund Policy instructed customers to submit refund requests to `info@thaitouristvisa.com`, an email address associated with a different domain, without clarifying its relationship to the TDAC service or the identified operating company.

Although the website disclosed its private status and published company information, these inconsistencies could leave customers uncertain about which legal entity was responsible for their payments, personal information, and refund requests.

The available evidence does not establish that the named company was fictitious or that the different contact addresses belonged to unrelated operators.

## Misleading Claims

The website previously published several inaccurate descriptions of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). Its homepage claimed the system launched in 2024, although Thailand officially introduced the TDAC on May 1, 2025. It also referred to obtaining a Thailand tourist visa during its application process, despite TDAC being an arrival-information form rather than a visa.

The website repeatedly described TDAC submissions as requiring government approval, promoted expedited processing, and advertised a 99.2% approval rate. These claims could mislead travelers into believing that TDAC applications undergo a discretionary approval process or that paid assistance provides special immigration advantages.

Furthermore, its FAQ promised TDAC approval 72 hours before departure, whereas Thailand's official registration window is calculated relative to arrival in Thailand.

Although some inaccuracies may reflect outdated content or poorly adapted templates, they could confuse travelers about official registration requirements, processing times, and the benefits of commercial assistance.
