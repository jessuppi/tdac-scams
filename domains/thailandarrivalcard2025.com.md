# thailandarrivalcard2025.com

Last updated: November 11, 2026

> [!NOTE]
> As of November 2026, this domain displays a parked

> activities, including information from previously
> published website pages and contemporary news reports.[^4][^5][^6][^7]

## Government Impersonation

The website previously promoted paid Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) registration through a domain closely associated with the official government program. Its homepage prominently described TDAC registration as mandatory and encouraged travelers to complete applications through its own commercial service, potentially creating confusion with Thailand's official immigration portal.[^8]

However, the website's About Us page and footer explicitly disclosed that it was a private intermediary, acknowledged its lack of government affiliation, and identified the free official registration service at `tdac.immigration.go.th`.[^1][^8][^9]

The concern is the potentially misleading impression created by its branding and application-focused presentation, rather than an explicit claim of government affiliation.

## Misleading Fees

The website previously advertised paid Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) assistance, despite official government registration being completely free. Its homepage advertised a Basic Plan costing $49.99 and an expedited service costing $69.99, with an additional $20 processing option.[^1][^8]

However, its separate Refund Policy advertised different prices: $59.99 for standard processing, $79.99 for fast processing, and $109.99 for ultra-premium processing. These inconsistent published prices could leave customers uncertain about the actual cost of the service.[^10]

Furthermore, the website's disclaimer stated that its commercial service fees were charged in addition to any applicable government fees, potentially suggesting that Thailand imposes a separate TDAC registration charge.[^12]

Although the operator disclosed that it provided optional paid assistance, these pricing inconsistencies and references to government fees risked misleading travelers about the cost of obtaining a TDAC. The available evidence does not establish what individual customers actually paid.

## Deceptive Promotion

The website previously promoted its paid TDAC service using claims of more than 30,000 successful approvals, years of immigration consulting experience, and thousands of completed applications. Its Refund Policy separately advertised a 99.2% approval rate without providing independently verifiable statistics or supporting methodology.[^8][^10]

The homepage also displayed favorable customer testimonials and promoted expedited processing, professional application reviews, and round-the-clock support. The identities and experiences of the quoted customers could not be independently verified.[^8]

These promotional claims may have exaggerated the advantages of purchasing commercial assistance for a government registration process that is available free of charge and typically takes approximately three to five minutes to complete through Thailand's official portal.

The absence of independent verification does not establish that the operator fabricated its customer testimonials or processing statistics.

## Hidden Disclosures

The website previously identified Lauksaimniecības SIA "DRUVA" as its operator, providing Latvian registration number 43903000810 and a registered address in Smiltene Municipality, Latvia.[^9]

Latvia's official Enterprise Register confirms that a company with this exact name, registration number, and address exists and was registered on March 14, 1992. However, public Latvian business records identify its activities as agriculture, including dairy farming, rather than immigration consulting.[^2][^3]

This discrepancy raises questions about the website's claimed corporate identity. Although the registration details are genuine, there is no independent evidence establishing that the Latvian company operated, controlled, or authorized the TDAC website.

The same Latvian company's name, registration number, and address also appeared on commercial arrival-card websites for Curaçao and Aruba. Their legal documents introduced additional company identities in Spain and Australia, respectively, despite displaying DRUVA's registration details elsewhere.[^14][^15][^16][^17][^18] These overlapping disclosures raise further questions about the use of the Latvian company's identity but do not independently establish common ownership, coordinated operations, or the company's involvement.

The website's Privacy Policy also referred to Thailand Travel Declaration LLC, while its footer displayed Thailand Agency Travel. The relationship between these different names was not explained.[^8][^11]

Furthermore, its Refund Policy directed customers to `info@thaitouristvisa.com`, an email address associated with another domain, without clarifying its relationship to the website or its claimed operator.[^10]

Another commercial TDAC website, `thaitouristvisa.com`, published a substantially similar Refund Policy, including identical service prices, approval-rate claims, refund conditions, and chargeback warnings.[^10][^19] The original website also directed refund requests to that domain's email address.[^10] However, the second website identified Switzerland's Weber GreenTech AG as its operator, rather than Latvia's DRUVA.[^9][^20] Weber GreenTech AG is an established environmental technology business, but its involvement in the TDAC service has not been independently verified.[^21] These similarities demonstrate overlapping website content and customer-contact information without establishing common ownership or either company's authorization.

Together, these inconsistencies could leave customers uncertain about which legal entity was responsible for payments, personal information, and refund requests. The available evidence does not establish whether the different names represented affiliated businesses, unrelated operators, or unauthorized use of a legitimate company's details.

## Misleading Claims

The website previously published several inaccurate descriptions of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). Its homepage claimed the system launched in 2024, although Thailand officially introduced the TDAC on May 1, 2025. It also referred to obtaining a Thailand tourist visa during its application process, despite TDAC being an arrival-information form rather than a visa.[^1][^8]

The website repeatedly described TDAC submissions as requiring government approval, promoted expedited processing, and advertised a 99.2% approval rate. These claims could mislead travelers into believing that TDAC applications undergo a discretionary approval process or that paid assistance provides special immigration advantages.[^8][^10]

Furthermore, its FAQ promised TDAC approval 72 hours before departure, whereas Thailand's official registration window is calculated relative to arrival in Thailand.[^1]

Although some inaccuracies may reflect outdated content or poorly adapted templates, they could confuse travelers about official registration requirements, processing times, and the benefits of commercial assistance.

## Privacy Concerns

The website previously requested sensitive passport information, personal details, and travel itineraries through its commercial TDAC application service. Its contact form also requested passport information from visitors making general inquiries, without clearly explaining why such details were necessary for questions unrelated to an existing application.[^13]

Its Privacy Policy disclosed the collection of contact information, payment details, account credentials, demographic information, and website usage data. It also permitted sharing information with third-party service providers and using customer information for promotional communications.[^11]

However, the policy did not specify a maximum retention period for sensitive application records or clearly explain how passport information was stored, retained, and deleted. The inconsistent company names appearing in its privacy disclosures also created uncertainty about responsibility for processing customer data.[^11]

Although these omissions raise legitimate privacy concerns, there is no independently verified evidence that the operator sold passport information, suffered a data breach, or otherwise misused customers' personal information.

## Billing or Service Issues

The website's previously published Refund Policy offered full refunds when TDAC applications were officially rejected by Thai immigration authorities. However, eligibility required official notification of rejection within 30 days of processing and a refund request supported by evidence within 60 days of rejection.[^10]

These conditions could make refunds difficult to obtain because Thailand's TDAC is an arrival-information submission rather than a conventional visa application subject to an ordinary approval or rejection process.[^1]

More concerningly, the policy discouraged customers from initiating chargebacks through their banks or payment providers, warning that doing so without first contacting the company could result in additional fees, account termination, or legal proceedings.[^10]

The policy also directed refund requests to an email address associated with a different domain, while its published service prices differed between the homepage and Refund Policy.[^8][^10]

Together, these provisions created uncertainty about refund eligibility, payment disputes, and customers' available remedies. The available evidence does not establish that the operator actually refused valid refunds, imposed penalties, or pursued legal action against customers.
